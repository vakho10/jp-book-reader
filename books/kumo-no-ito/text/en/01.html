I

It happened one day. The Buddha was strolling alone along the edge of the lotus pond in Paradise. The lotus blossoms in the pond were all as white as pearls, and from the golden stamens at their hearts an indescribably sweet fragrance drifted out ceaselessly all around. It must have been morning in Paradise.

Presently the Buddha paused at the edge of the pond and, through a gap between the lotus leaves that covered the water, happened to glance at what lay below. Directly beneath this lotus pond of Paradise lies the very bottom of Hell, so through the crystal-clear water the River of Three Crossings and the Mountain of Needles could be seen sharply, just as if through the lens of a peep-box.

Then, down at the bottom of Hell, his eye fell on a man named Kandata, writhing there among the other sinners. This Kandata was a great robber who had done all manner of evil, killing people and setting houses on fire, yet even he could remember one good deed. It was this: once, as he was passing through a deep forest, he saw a tiny spider creeping along the side of the path. Kandata at once raised his foot to stamp on it, but then he thought better of it. “No, no. Small as it is, this too must be a living thing. To take its life for no reason would be a pity, whatever else I’ve done.” And so in the end he spared the spider and let it live.

As he watched the scene in Hell, the Buddha remembered that this Kandata had once saved a spider. And he thought that, as a reward for that one good deed, he would rescue the man from Hell if he could. Fortunately, when he looked beside him, there on a lotus leaf the color of jade was a spider of Paradise, spinning a beautiful silver thread. The Buddha gently took the spider’s thread in his hand and lowered it straight down, between the pearl-white lotuses, to the bottom of Hell far below.
