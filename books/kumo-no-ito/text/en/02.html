II

Meanwhile, down in the Pool of Blood at the bottom of Hell, Kandata was bobbing up and sinking down along with the other sinners. Wherever he looked it was pitch dark, and whenever something did loom faintly out of the darkness, it was only the glint of the needles on the dreadful Mountain of Needles, so his loneliness was beyond words. On top of that, everything around him was as hushed as the inside of a tomb, and the only sound to be heard now and then was the faint sighing of the sinners. Anyone who had fallen this far, it seems, was so worn out by the many torments of Hell that they no longer had the strength even to cry. So even Kandata, great robber though he was, could only choke on the blood of the pool and struggle like a dying frog.

Then one day it happened. Kandata idly raised his head and looked up at the sky above the Pool of Blood, and there, through the silent darkness, from the far, far heavens, came a silver spider’s thread, a single fine glinting strand, sliding smoothly down toward him as if it feared being seen. When Kandata saw it, he clapped his hands for joy before he knew it. If he clung to this thread and climbed and kept on climbing, he was sure to get out of Hell. Why, with luck he might even get into Paradise. Then he would never again be driven up the Mountain of Needles, nor sunk in the Pool of Blood.

With this in mind, Kandata immediately seized the spider’s thread firmly in both hands and began hauling himself up, hand over hand, with all his might. Being a great robber, he had of course long been well practiced at this sort of thing.

But between Hell and Paradise lie tens of thousands of leagues, so no matter how he strained, it was not easy to reach the top. After climbing for quite a while, Kandata at last grew tired and could not pull himself up a single hand’s length more. Having no other choice, he decided to rest for a moment, and hanging there halfway up the thread, he looked far down below him.

And sure enough, his efforts had paid off: the Pool of Blood where he had been until a moment ago was now hidden somewhere in the depths of the darkness. That dreadful, faintly glinting Mountain of Needles, too, was now beneath his feet. If he kept climbing at this rate, getting out of Hell might be easier than he had thought. Twining both hands in the spider’s thread, Kandata laughed in a voice he had not used in all his years down there. “I’ve done it! I’ve done it!” But then he suddenly noticed that, on the thread below him, countless sinners were following in his wake, climbing up and up just as single-mindedly as he, like a line of ants. When Kandata saw this, he was so startled and frightened that for a while he could only hang there with his mouth gaping like a fool, his eyes darting about. How could this slender spider’s thread, which looked as if it might snap under him alone, possibly bear the weight of so many people? If by some chance it broke along the way, even he himself, who mattered most and had climbed all this way, would have to plunge headlong back into Hell. That would be a disaster. Yet even as he thought this, the sinners came swarming up by the hundreds and the thousands out of the pitch-dark Pool of Blood, and in a single file they climbed busily up the thin, glinting spider’s thread. Unless he did something now, the thread was sure to snap in two in the middle and fall.

So Kandata raised his voice and bellowed. “Hey, you sinners! This spider’s thread is mine! Who said you could climb up here? Get down! Get down!”

That was the very moment. The spider’s thread, which until then had held without the slightest trouble, suddenly snapped with a twang right at the point where Kandata was hanging. Kandata could do nothing. Before he could so much as cry out, he was cutting through the wind, spinning round and round like a top, and in the blink of an eye he plunged headfirst into the depths of the darkness.

All that was left behind was the spider’s thread of Paradise, glinting finely as it hung, short, in the middle of a sky without moon or stars.
